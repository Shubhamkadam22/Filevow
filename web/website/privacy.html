Privacy policy

Last updated: 24 November 2026

The short version: FileVow runs entirely on your own computer. We don't run a cloud service, we don't collect analytics, and the app doesn't send us your files, your account details or information about how you use it.

1. Who this policy covers

This policy covers the FileVow desktop app and this website. The person or organisation that installs FileVow on a computer (for example, you, or your employer on a work PC) is responsible for the data FileVow keeps on that computer.

2. What the app stores on your computer

InformationWhyWhere it's kept
Your username and a hash of your password (Argon2id)To sign you in. Your actual password is never stored.FileVow's database in your user folder

By default this is the %LOCALAPPDATA%\FileVow folder of your Windows account. FileVow's own encryption keys are kept in the Windows Credential Manager.

3. What leaves your computer

Nothing, unless you set it up. If you add a notification channel (email, Slack, Microsoft Teams or a webhook), FileVow sends alert messages to the service you chose. Those messages contain the alert title, severity, file path and time. They never contain file contents. The service you choose handles that information under its own privacy policy.

FileVow doesn't check for updates in the background, doesn't show ads and contains no tracking or analytics code.

4. Other people on the same computer

Each FileVow account has a private workspace. Other accounts, including the administrator account, can't see your protected files, changes, alerts or audit log in the app. The administrator can create, lock and remove accounts. Anyone with full administrator rights on Windows itself can technically access files on that computer, which is true of any desktop software.

5. This website

This website is a set of static pages. It doesn't use cookies, analytics or third-party trackers, and it doesn't ask for any personal information. Like any web server, the hosting provider may keep standard access logs (such as IP address, time and page requested) for security and reliability.

6. How long information is kept

FileVow keeps its records until you remove them. Removing a folder from monitoring stops it being watched; uninstalling FileVow and deleting its data folder removes everything it stored. Audit log entries are kept by design, because a history that can be quietly shortened can't be trusted.

7. Your choices

  • Choose exactly which folders and files FileVow watches, and stop watching them at any time.
  • Turn notification channels on or off; none are on by default.
  • Export your audit log as CSV.
  • Delete everything by uninstalling FileVow and removing its data folder.

8. Children

FileVow is a general-purpose utility and isn't directed at children. It doesn't knowingly collect information from anyone, because it doesn't collect information at all.

9. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we'll update the date at the top of this page and describe significant changes in the release notes of the next version.

10. Contact

Questions about privacy? Contact the FileVow team at privacy@filevow.app.
