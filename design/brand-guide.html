The mark

One symbol, no wordmark. Three nested hexagons stand for FileVow's three verification layers, sealed around a check mark. The hexagon is the shape of strength and structure, which suits integrity, and it avoids the overused padlock and shield.

Outer hexagon: the mirror. The encrypted reference copy that surrounds and protects everything.
Middle hexagon: the RSA signature. The seal that authenticates the record. It is the lighter blue ring.
Solid core: the SHA-256 fingerprint. The file's identity at the centre.
Check mark: the vow is kept. All three layers verified.

Below 32 px the rings blur together, so use favicon.svg (solid hexagon and check). Keep clear space around the mark equal to one ring gap (about 1/8 of its width). Never stretch, rotate, recolour outside the palette, or add shadows or gradients.

Colour palette

Navy and blue are the colours people most associate with trust, security and reliability; banks, governments and security vendors use them for that reason. The palette is deliberately calm: mostly neutral slate, navy for structure, and colour only where it means something.

Rules for a trustworthy interface

  • Calm by default. White cards on a light slate page, navy structure. No gradients, glows, neon or animations for decoration.
  • Colour means something. Green is verified, amber is pending, red is critical, and nothing else uses those colours.
  • Never colour alone. Every status has a word and an icon as well.
  • Show the proof. Hashes, key fingerprints and timestamps appear in monospace, so users can see the evidence instead of being asked to believe it.
  • Consistent and plain. One font family, a 4 px spacing grid, 6 px corners, the same badge shapes everywhere. Predictable interfaces feel reliable.
  • Accessible. All text meets WCAG AA contrast. Focus outlines are always visible (blue 2 px ring).
